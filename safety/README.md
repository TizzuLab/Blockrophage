# Safety sheet for the Blockrophage course

> **Under construction.** This course runs for the first time in spring 2027 and is being built in the open. Interested students are welcome to apply and to give input ([contact](https://zindellab.ch/contact.html), [issues](https://github.com/TizzuLab/blockrophage-course/issues)). **Please do not use these protocols in the lab yet.** Values are unverified until this notice is gone.

Two pages. Read them before the first day, bring them to the safety briefing on
Thursday 18 March 2027, 09:00, and sign the last section after the briefing. The
signed sheet is your admission to the bench.

## 1. What you should have read already

- **The ETH Zürich biosafety concept** for level 1 and 2 laboratories, published by
  Safety, Security, Health and Environment (SSHE):
  <https://ethz.ch/content/dam/ethz/associates/services/Service/sicherheit-gesundheit-umwelt/files/biosicherheit_neu/en/Biosafety_concept.pdf>.
  It is the framework for every lab at ETH. If you have not read it, do it now; the
  first thirteen pages are the concept, the rest are the annexes you will meet
  below `[TODO: check the SSHE page for a newer version than January 2017]`.
- **The IMHS safety concept 2026**, the binding document for the rooms you work in.
  It is an internal document of the Institute of Molecular Health Sciences and is
  sent to you by email with your enrolment confirmation. It follows the ETH
  concept section by section and adds the institute's rules, contacts and waste
  plan.

Both say the same thing about you: the group leader or a delegate instructs every
new person in the lab, and the instruction is recorded in writing (ETH concept,
section 6.3; IMHS concept, sections 3.3.1 and 6.3). The briefing and your
signature below are that record.

## 2. Specifically for this course you need to know

**Where and when.** HPL, F floor. Course hours 09:00 to 17:00; there is no work in
the lab outside these hours and no overnight presence; overnight steps run in the
incubator without you. The briefing walks you through the emergency exits, the
eyewash and emergency shower, the first-aid kit, the spill kit and the fire
extinguisher of the floor.

**Biosafety level.** The core programme is level 1 work: RAW 264.7 is a mouse cell
line, not a pathogen and not a genetically modified organism, and the stimuli are
purified molecules `[TODO: confirm the classification of the course lab on HPL F]`.
Optional extensions that use surplus primary human or mouse material are level 2
work: they take place only in the level 2 rooms, only with a supervisor present,
and human material is handled as potentially infectious according to the rules for
blood-transmissible diseases (ETH concept, annex 7; IMHS concept, section 6.2.4).

**Personal protection and conduct.** Lab coat closed, gloves at the hood and with
every chemical below, closed shoes, long hair tied. Coat and gloves stay in the
lab: not in the seminar room, the office, the toilet or the corridor. No food, no
drink, no phone in the hand that holds the pipette. Wash your hands when you leave.
Tell the course lead in confidence if you are pregnant, immunocompromised or
allergic to latex or nitrile; the work can be adapted without anyone else knowing.

**The biosafety cabinet.** Switch it on and let it run before you start; keep the
front grille free; wipe the surface with 70% ethanol before and after; no open
flame; slow movements; everything you need goes in before you begin, so that you
are not reaching out and in. When the alarm sounds, finish the step and call your
supervisor (IMHS concept, section 6.2.2).

**Chemicals of this course**, with the one rule each that matters.

| Reagent | Where | Hazard | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| DMSO (freezing medium) | P03 | carries whatever is dissolved in it through skin | gloves; change them at once on contact |
| MTT | P09 | suspected mutagen, light sensitive | weigh in the hood, gloves, solid and liquid waste as chemical waste |
| Acid isopropanol (0.04 M HCl) | P09 | flammable, mildly corrosive | closed bottle, no flame, chemical waste |
| Trypan blue | P01, P02 | suspected carcinogen | gloves; chemical waste, not the sink |
| Liquid nitrogen | P03 | cold burns; vials can burst on thawing | cryo gloves and face shield when a vial leaves the tank |
| Griess reagent (sulfanilamide, NED, phosphoric acid) | P07 | irritant, acidic | gloves, goggles when pouring |
| ELISA stop solution (acid) | P06 | corrosive | gloves, goggles, multichannel pipette, chemical waste |
| LPS and PRR ligands | P04, P05 | endotoxin; no hazard at the microgram amounts used | do not aerosolise; gloves |
| 70% ethanol | everywhere | flammable | spray bottles away from flames and hot plates |
| PFA 4% | X03 | toxic fixative | hood only, PFA waste container, staff handle it |
| DAB | X03 | suspected carcinogen | staff handle it; DAB waste container |

**Sharps.** The core programme uses no needles and no blades. Scalpels appear only in
the histology extension, in the hands of staff. Anything sharp goes into the solid
sharps container, never into a bag.

**Waste.** Four streams, following the IMHS disposal plan (section 6.5): liquid
culture waste (medium, cells, supernatants you no longer need) into the
disinfectant container at the hood `[TODO: HPL procedure and disinfectant]`;
plastics that touched cells into the biological waste bag; the chemicals above into
the labelled chemical waste containers; everything else is municipal waste. When
you are not sure, ask; nothing goes down the sink except water and buffers without
cells or chemicals.

**Incidents.** A cut, a stab, a splash in the eye or on broken skin: rinse at once,
eyes for fifteen minutes at the eyewash, then tell your supervisor immediately, even
for a minor injury. Every incident is written up on the IMHS report sheet with your
supervisor the same day (IMHS concept, section 4.3). Emergency numbers from a lab
phone: **888** for the ETH Emergency Desk, **0-144** for the ambulance, **0-118** for
the fire brigade; the external numbers are on the sign at every lab door. Alarm
first, then the supervisor, then the course lead.
